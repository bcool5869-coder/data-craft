The data told a quiet story. Every morning the cafe opened at seven, and every morning the first customer ordered a latte.
The owner did not notice the pattern until she wrote the orders down. Then she counted them, and the counts made a table,
and the table made a chart, and the chart showed the weekends rising like small hills.
A model is a simple idea. It looks at many examples and learns a rule that fits them. When the rule is good, it works on
new examples too. When the rule is too clever, it remembers the old examples and fails on the new ones.
The student asked the teacher why the loss went down. The teacher said that each step moved the weights a little
downhill, and after thousands of small steps the model found the bottom of the valley.
Tokens are small pieces of text. The model reads the tokens one by one and guesses the next one. It is wrong at first.
Then it is trained, and it becomes less wrong, and one day it writes a sentence that sounds almost like a person.
Clean the data first. Look at it carefully. Ask a clear question. Test the answer on data the model has never seen.
Tell the truth about what the numbers say, and about what they do not say.
